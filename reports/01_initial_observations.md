
# Initial Geochemical Observations

## Dataset and analytical measurements

The dataset comprises 103 in-situ analytical measurements of phosphatic components from the Early Cambrian Kakul Garlaniyan phosphorites in northeastern Pakistan (Ali et al., 2026). The measurements were obtained from the lower (LPL), middle (MPL), and upper (UPL) phosphorite layers.

The analyzed components include 35 oolite-core, 27 oolite-rim, 20 pellet-core, and 21 pellet-rim measurements. The data were obtained from Supplementary Table S2 of the published study.

Total rare earth element and yttrium (REY) concentrations range from 222.77 to 922.74 ppm, with a mean of 587.87 ppm and a standard deviation of 152.21 ppm.

*Figure 1. Distribution of total REY concentrations among the lower (LPL), middle (MPL), and upper (UPL) phosphorite layers of the Kakul Garlaniyan section. Boxes represent the interquartile range, central lines indicate medians, whiskers extend to the most extreme observations within 1.5 times the interquartile range, and circles indicate observations beyond the whiskers.*

Figure 1 illustrates differences in the distribution of total REY concentrations among the three phosphorite layers. The UPL displays a higher median concentration than the LPL and MPL, although the distributions overlap.

These observations describe the measured variation among the layers and do not establish statistically significant differences or their geological causes.

Boxes represent the interquartile range, central lines indicate medians, whiskers extend to the most extreme observations within 1.5 times the interquartile range, and circles indicate observations beyond the whiskers.*

The mean total REY concentrations of oolites and pellets are summarized in Table 1.

**Table 1. Mean total REY concentrations in oolites and pellets from the three phosphorite layers.**

| Phosphorite layer | Oolites (ppm) | Pellets (ppm) |
|---|---:|---:|
| LPL | 648.81 | 488.60 |
| MPL | 612.02 | 425.33 |
| UPL | 704.21 | 500.37 |

Oolites exhibit higher mean total REY concentrations than pellets in all three phosphorite layers. This pattern is consistent with the descriptive grain-type differences documented in the published study (Ali et al., 2026).

However, the comparisons are based on individual analytical spots, and some measurements may belong to the same parent rock. Therefore, the observed differences should not be interpreted as independent-rock effects without verifying the sample identifiers and accounting for the grouping of measurements.

## Limitations of the initial analysis

The present analysis describes the distribution of REY concentrations across stratigraphic layers and phosphatic components. It does not independently determine the processes responsible for REY enrichment.

Further evaluation is necessary to assess the consistency of grain-type differences within parent rocks, the relationships with measured mineral chemistry, and the reliability of any predictive models.
